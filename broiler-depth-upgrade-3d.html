01

Why upgrade to depth

Weight lives in volume, not in a flat silhouette. A 2D camera has to guess the third dimension through calibration; a depth camera measures it directly, per pixel, in millimetres.

What v1 (2D · Tapo 530CW) can't see

  • Scale is assumed, not measured — the mm/pixel factor depends on exact mount height; a 10 cm error skews every area reading.
  • Litter build-up shifts the baseline — as litter rises, the real camera-to-bird distance changes but the calibration doesn't.
  • Posture distorts area — wing spread, crouching and pecking change the 2D silhouette by 10–20% with zero weight change.
  • Touching birds merge — overlapping silhouettes defeat ellipse segmentation in dense stocking.
  • Light-dependent — needs uniform lighting; dark periods produce weak samples.

What a depth camera adds

  • Metric 3D per pixel — every point has (x, y, z) in mm from factory intrinsics. No height calibration, ever.
  • True volume & height profile — the strongest single predictors of body weight, measured directly.
  • Self-zeroing floor — the litter plane is re-fit automatically (RANSAC), so litter top-ups don't drift the model.
  • Separates touching birds — height discontinuities split clusters that look merged in 2D.
  • Works in the dark — active IR stereo keeps sampling through low-light and night periods.

Keep mounts ≤ 3.5 m so edge pixels stay well inside the accurate depth range. Weight sampling is statistical — 2–4 cameras per house at feeder/drinker zones is enough; full-floor coverage is not required.

Depth accuracy at bird scale

  • At 3.0 m mount height, per-pixel footprint is roughly 3–4 mm — fine enough to resolve back curvature.
  • Depth noise at 3 m is typically < 1–2% of distance; averaging over a bird's ~10–20k surface points reduces it further.
  • Absolute scale means one shared weight model works across all sheds and mount heights — no per-site pixel calibration.

04

Weight model — volume-first

v1 regressed weight from 2D area/perimeter in converted pixels. v2 regresses from directly measured 3D quantities, with the 2D-era features kept only as secondary inputs.

// per validated standing sample
Weight (kg) = β₀ + β₁·V + β₂·A_back + β₃·h_max + β₄·L₃D + β₅·W₃D + GBM(residual)
// V = voxel-integrated volume above litter plane · A_back = 3D back surface area
// h_max = max height above plane · L₃D, W₃D = metric body length / width

Expected accuracy

  • Depth-based broiler weighing in published trials typically reaches ±2–4% (≈ 40–80 g on a 2 kg bird) vs ±8–12% for 2D area methods.
  • Daily shed median over hundreds of samples tightens flock-level error further.

Training & calibration

  • Collect paired samples: platform-scale weights + synchronized point clouds, across grow-out ages.
  • One model per breed (Ross 308 / Cobb 500); retrain per flock only if drift alerts fire.
  • Weekly hand-weigh spot checks feed a drift monitor — same SOP cadence as v1.

Flock outputs

  • Daily median weight, CV% uniformity, and growth curve vs breed standard.
  • Deviation alerts route through the existing management-by-exception NOC flow.

07

Deployment considerations

Mounting & cabling

  • Nadir mount at 2.5–3.5 m, perpendicular to floor — unchanged from v1 discipline.
  • OAK-D Pro PoE: one outdoor-rated Cat6 run per camera back to the PoE switch; no shed-side power.
  • Place cameras over feeder/drinker lines where standing birds concentrate.

Environment & SOPs

  • IP67 body survives washdown; add a simple drip shield above the lens window.
  • Fold lens wipe-down into the existing camera lens-cleaning / washdown SOP checklist — same cadence, one more line item.
  • GPU box lives in the office/NOC room behind dust filters + UPS, never inside the shed.

Validation & rollout

  • Phase 1: one house, D455 + OAK-D side-by-side vs platform scale for one full grow-out.
  • Phase 2: lock the model, fit remaining houses with OAK-D Pro PoE only.
  • Weekly hand-weigh spot checks + automated drift alerts keep the model honest.
